The Buendía family lives through generations of love, war, and magic in the town of Macondo, where time seems to fold in on itself.

## Protagonists

- **José Arcadio Buendía** — the founder of Macondo, obsessed with inventions, maps, and impossible knowledge.
- **Úrsula Iguarán** — the matriarch who holds the family together for a century with will and memory.
- **Colonel Aureliano Buendía** — a son who goes to war so many times that war becomes his weather.
- **Remedios the Beauty** — so otherworldly that the book treats her like a myth walking through the kitchen.
- **Aureliano Babilonia** — one of the last, trying to read the family's encoded fate.

## Plot

Macondo is founded, grows, is visited by gypsies, banana companies, rain that lasts years, and loves that repeat under new names. Sons are named after fathers. Mistakes return. García Márquez does not tell one life. He tells a lineage until the town and the manuscript catch up with each other.

## Themes

Time loops. Memory fails and then becomes legend. Colonial greed, civil war, and solitude run through the bloodline. Magical realism here is not decoration: rain, ghosts, and flying carpets sit next to political violence as if both were ordinary.

## Review

Dense, musical, and worth slowing down for. The repeating names are confusing on purpose. Once you accept that the family is the protagonist, the book opens. A novel that feels like listening to someone remember a whole country.
