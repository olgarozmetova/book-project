# The Catcher in the Rye

## Protagonists

Holden Caulfield is a teenage narrator who hates "phonies," talks in circles, and is more lost than he admits. Phoebe Caulfield is his little sister, the one person who still feels real to him. Sally Hayes is a girl from his old life; Holden wants an escape hatch and she wants a normal date. Mr. Antolini is a former teacher who tries to help, in a scene that leaves Holden even more unsure.

## Plot

After another expulsion, Holden does not go home. He drifts through New York: hotels, bars, museums, a date that collapses, a visit to Phoebe. He wants to freeze time for children and cannot freeze it for himself. The book is less a plot than a few days inside a breaking mind.

## Themes

Salinger is writing grief dressed up as attitude. Holden's anger at adults is also fear of growing up, of sex, of death, of becoming fake. The catcher in the rye is an image of protection: save the kids before they fall off the cliff. He cannot even save himself.

## Review

The voice is the whole trick, and it still works. Holden is funny, exhausting, and sad in the same paragraph. If you have ever been sixteen and sure everyone else is performing, this book will feel uncomfortably close.
