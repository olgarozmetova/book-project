# Pride and Prejudice

## Protagonists

Elizabeth Bennet is witty, stubborn, and sure she can read people, though she is wrong about a few important ones. Fitzwilliam Darcy is rich, awkward, proud, and much kinder than his first impression. Jane Bennet is Elizabeth's gentle older sister, in love with Mr. Bingley. Mr. Bingley is easy-going and rich, too easily steered by his friends. Mr. Wickham is charming on the surface and disastrous underneath. Mrs. Bennet is a mother whose panic about marriage would be comic if the stakes were not real.

## Plot

A new wealthy neighbor arrives, dances happen, and pride does the rest. Elizabeth rejects a proposal she finds insulting. Letters, scandals, and a visit to Pemberley force both her and Darcy to admit they judged too fast. Austen makes a love story out of people learning to see clearly.

## Themes

Class, money, and marriage are the machinery of the plot. The deeper subject is judgment: how wit can become prejudice, and how pride can look like coldness. Happy endings still depend on income. Austen never pretends otherwise.

## Review

Funny, sharp, and still easy to reread. The dialogue does most of the work. Elizabeth remains one of the best heroines in English fiction because she is allowed to be wrong and then change. A ten out of ten comfort book that is smarter than it looks.
