In a totalitarian future, Winston Smith tries to keep a private thought alive under the gaze of Big Brother and the Party.

## Protagonists

- **Winston Smith** — a worker at the Ministry of Truth who rewrites history and starts to hate the lie he lives inside.
- **Julia** — a young woman who looks loyal to the Party and quietly breaks its rules.
- **O'Brien** — a Party insider who seems like an ally and becomes something much worse.
- **Big Brother** — the face of the regime. He may not even be a real person, which is part of the point.

## Plot

Oceania is watched, starved, and told what to remember. Winston buys a diary, falls in love, and looks for a way out of doublethink. The Party does not only punish rebellion. It wants to own the inside of the mind.

## Themes

Orwell is writing about language as a weapon: if you shrink the words, you shrink the thoughts. Surveillance, fear, and rewritten history turn people into tools. The scariest idea is not prison. It is believing the Party after it has broken you.

## Review

Still sharp decades later. The slogans stick, the love story is brief and human, and the last third is brutal. It is not subtle, and it does not need to be. A book you finish and then notice in the news.
